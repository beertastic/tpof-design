# Production Design Bible v1.0

## 1. Creative Vision

- OT/Andor-inspired realism with a subtle *The Mandalorian* frontier aesthetic.
- Practical costume construction.
- Weathered materials.
- Realistic proportions.
- Functional technology.
- Consistent photography.
- Consistent lighting.
- Consistent skin rendering.
- Shared level of detail.

---

## 2. Design Philosophy

> **This is a galaxy built from industrial salvage. Nothing is wasted, everything has a previous owner, and every object carries the marks of the lives that came before it.**

### Function over Decoration

Everything should have a believable purpose. Clothing, armour, tools, and vehicles are designed for work first and appearance second.

### History is Visible

Nothing is factory fresh. Every object shows evidence of use through repairs, scratches, faded paint, worn fabric, or replaced components.

### Real-world Construction

Costumes should appear as if they could be manufactured using believable techniques and materials. Avoid impossible seams, floating armour pieces, or purely decorative construction.

### Occupation Shapes Appearance

A person's clothing should immediately communicate their profession. Mercenary captains, mechanics, navigators, boarding specialists, engineers, and grunts should all have distinct silhouettes and equipment while clearly belonging to the same universe.

### Resourcefulness

People repair rather than replace. Equipment is modified, repurposed, upgraded, and adapted over years of use.

### Practical Technology

Technology should look engineered and maintainable, with visible access panels, fasteners, vents, exposed mechanisms, and evidence of servicing.

### Imperfection Creates Authenticity

Asymmetry, patched fabrics, dents, faded markings, and subtle manufacturing variations make the world feel lived in.

### Mercenary Identity

Characters should evoke the feel of:

- Outer Rim raiders
- Salvage mercenaries
- Independent corsairs
- Boarding specialists
- Smugglers-for-hire
- Freelance raiders
- Frontier marauders

Avoid obvious Earth pirate stereotypes.

## 3. Colour Palette

The overall palette is muted, sun-faded, and practical. Bright colours are uncommon and reserved for rank, warning markings, cultural identity, or significant personal items.

### Primary Colours

- Charcoal
- Ash grey
- Weathered black
- Faded olive
- Dust brown
- Sand
- Bone white
- Cream

### Secondary Colours

- Rust red
- Ochre
- Deep burgundy
- Forest green
- Navy blue

### Accent Colours

Used sparingly.

- Brass
- Copper
- Oxidised bronze
- Imperial red
- Warning yellow
- Signal blue

Colours should appear naturally faded through age, sunlight, and repeated cleaning.

---

## 4. Costume Language

Clothing should feel like functional workwear adapted for a dangerous life aboard ships and frontier settlements.

Garments are assembled from salvaged military surplus, industrial workwear, flight gear, and locally manufactured textiles.

Visible Earth historical fashion should be avoided.

### Preferred Influences

- Flight suits
- Mechanic coveralls
- Modular armour panels
- Utility harnesses
- Padded work jackets
- Weather capes
- Survival gear
- Pressure garments
- Industrial gloves

### Avoid

- Pirate coats
- Tricorn silhouettes
- Corsets
- Renaissance clothing
- Medieval leather armour
- Fantasy shoulder pads
- Decorative belts
- Swashbuckler aesthetics

---

## 5. Materials

Materials should always feel tangible and physically believable.

### Preferred Materials

- Ballistic weave
- Quilted flight fabric
- Reinforced synth-leather
- Flexible armour mesh
- Woven technical textiles
- Plastoid armour plates
- Insulated work fabric
- Industrial rubber
- Carbon-fibre composites
- Brushed alloy

Surfaces should display wear, repairs, scratches, grime, fading, and evidence of repeated use.

---

## 6. Weapons

Weapons are tools that have evolved alongside their owners.

Every weapon should show evidence of long-term use through:

- Repaired grips
- Scratched finishes
- Replacement components
- Personalised slings
- Improvised modifications
- Field repairs

Weapons should feel mechanically believable, robust, and maintainable.

Avoid oversized, highly stylised, ceremonial, or ornamental designs.

---

## 6.5 Earth Influence Guidelines

Designs should never appear to originate from a recognisable period of Earth history.

### Avoid Direct References

- Caribbean pirates
- Medieval Europe
- Victorian fashion
- Roman armour

### Subtle Inspiration Is Acceptable

Use only broad functional ideas—not recognisable costumes—from:

- Wild West frontier gear
- Viking practicality
- Industrial workwear
- Military surplus
- Aviation clothing
- Exploration equipment

Everything should ultimately feel native to the Star Wars universe.

---

## 7. Vehicles

Vehicles are working machines first and symbols second.

Every vehicle should display:

- Maintenance access panels
- Replacement parts
- Mismatched paint
- Service markings
- Fuel stains
- Practical cargo solutions

Interiors should feel cramped, functional, and lived in rather than luxurious.

Repairs should often be visible rather than hidden.

---

## 8. Architecture

Buildings are designed around necessity rather than aesthetics.

Construction should favour practical engineering using locally available materials and salvaged components.

Structures should show evidence of:

- Expansion
- Repair
- Adaptation
- Repeated occupation

Common visual features include:

- Exposed pipework
- Maintenance access
- Weather damage
- Reinforced supports
- Improvised additions

Avoid pristine futuristic minimalism.

---

## 9. Lighting

Lighting should always feel motivated by believable sources.

### Exterior

- Natural sunlight
- Overcast skies
- Planetary atmospheres

### Interior

- Work lamps
- Bulkhead fixtures
- Warning lights
- Control panels
- Practical industrial lighting

Avoid dramatic coloured lighting unless it serves a clear narrative purpose.

---

## 10. Cinematography

Images should resemble frames from a live-action Star Wars production.

Naturalistic composition is preferred over heroic posing.

Use:

- Restrained colour grading
- Subtle atmospheric haze
- Realistic depth of field
- Fine film grain
- Practical lens behaviour

Characters should frequently be engaged in meaningful activity rather than looking directly at the camera.

The world should always feel larger than the people within it.

---

## 11. Species Design

Alien species should feel biologically plausible and grounded within live-action Star Wars.

Costumes should adapt naturally to each species' anatomy without sacrificing practicality.

Skin textures, horns, lekku, prosthetics, and facial features should favour practical make-up effects over exaggerated digital fantasy.

Alien cultures should express individuality through clothing, tools, customs, and craftsmanship rather than extreme anatomy alone.

---

## 12. Prompt Rules

Every prompt should follow this hierarchy:

1. Apply the **Style Bible**.
2. Apply the relevant **Faction Guide**.
3. Apply the **Character Sheet**.
4. Apply the **Scene Description**.
5. Apply the **Camera Direction**.

The Style Bible always takes precedence unless explicitly overridden.

---

## 13. Audience Layers

This film is designed to work at three levels at once, and no design decision may
serve one at the expense of another.

**The newcomer.** Someone who has never heard of either lead must be able to
watch a complete, self-contained story: a Jedi in hiding, a captive girl, and a
massacre in a clearing. If they leave saying *"that Jedi saved that girl"*, the
film has worked.

**The casual fan.** They should reach the end and get the reveal — *that's
Baylan?* — having not seen it coming. This is why he reads as a mercenary
throughout and why the name only appears at the climax.

**The hard-core fan.** They should spot `Bay` → `Baylan` early and spend the film
knowing more than everyone else. Shin is harder for them — red hair and a
character who behaves nothing like the woman they know — which is deliberate.

### Practical rules

- **Never signal early.** No design element may confirm either identity before
  the film chooses to.
- **Build to the end.** Recognition should be strongest in the final scene and
  weakest in the first.
- **Never nod to camera.** No design element exists solely as a reference.
- **Divergence is permitted and expected.** Shin's red hair and Baylan's
  yellow blade differ from what returning viewers expect. Those gaps are
  deliberate and are not errors to be corrected.
