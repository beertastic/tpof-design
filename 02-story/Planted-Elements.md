# Planted Elements

Things established in **The Price of Freedom** that pay off in Films 2 and 3.

> **Why this document exists.** "Not important now" is exactly when things get
> designed carelessly. A background prop gets whatever is to hand, nobody
> photographs it, and three years later it is a hero object that cannot be
> matched. Everything listed here must be **designed properly now, referenced
> properly now, and archived** — regardless of how little screen time it has.

Each entry states what the art department has to do **in this film**.

---

## P1 — "Don't let the fear control you"

**Planted:** Jeyin says it to Shin, more than once, across this film. Likely her
central line.

**Pays off:** In Films 2–3, **Baylan teaches the opposite** — that fear is a
tool, to be used rather than resisted. Not Sith, but a direct contradiction of
her dead mother. Shin is caught between the two people who raised her, one of
whom is not there to argue.

**This is the thematic spine of the trilogy**, and it is set up entirely by a
woman who dies in Film 1.

### Required in this film

- **The line lands as a refrain earlier in the film**, more than once, then is
  **echoed unfinished as she dies** (confirmed 2026-07-30). She starts it and does
  not complete it.
- Shin is therefore left with **an instruction that stops halfway** — so Baylan is
  not contradicting a completed statement in Film 2, he is filling a sentence her
  mother never got to finish.
- **Shin's visible fear is thematic, not incidental.** She is frightened in
  almost every scene and it reads plainly on her — that is now the *subject*, not
  characterisation. Every image of her being afraid is planting.
- Nothing in Shin's design may suggest she is mastering her fear. She is not.
  That is Film 2's business.

[`03-characters/shin/`](../03-characters/shin/)

## P2 — Vala survives

**Planted:** Vala volunteers to lead the hunters away in Scene 22. Her fight with
Nyx **ends with a grenade detonating** and the audience believes she was blown up.
**She survives — barely — and a post-credit scene shows it.**

The grenade is what makes the plant work: an explosion reads as a death without
producing a body. **She survives because she is dropped and rolls clear on arena
reflex** — trained instinct firing against her own accepted decision to die. The
roll must read as a falling body on first viewing and as a deliberate act on
rewatch, and she needs established cover to reach.

**Pays off:** In Film 2, Baylan and Shin encounter her again. Shin is angry that
Vala left, and in Shin's head Vala is part of the reason Jeyin died. They fight —
either at Baylan's command, or because Vala attacks Baylan and Shin defends him.
**Shin kills her.**

Shin loses a piece of herself doing it: Vala was a source of comfort, and the
person who gave her the one object she owns. Afterwards she looks at Vala's
clasp — and keeps it. She keeps Vala with her anyway.

### Required in this film

- **Do not stage a death.** Her Scene 22/23 exit must read as *gone*, not
  *killed*. No body, no confirmed kill, no dialogue asserting she is dead.
  Others may believe it; the film must not confirm it.
- **Her injuries are deferred to Film 2** (2026-07-30). The requirement on *this*
  film is only that nothing contradicts a future decision: no injury is shown, no
  dialogue describes her condition, and her exit leaves her state entirely open.
- Whatever is chosen later must be readable as the cost of surviving — the
  audience should understand what it took without being told.
- **The post-credit scene is an asset for this film** and needs a location, a
  costume state and a makeup state. It is not a Film 2 problem.
- **Archive her costume.** It continues into Film 2 in a damaged state.
- **The clasp must be built to last.** See P3.

**See:** [`03-characters/vala/`](../03-characters/vala/),
[`03-characters/shin/`](../03-characters/shin/)

## P2b — The akk dog survives too

**Planted:** Shin knocks it out with a Force blast. Baylan will not kill it —
he has fed it for eighteen years — so it is left unconscious in the clearing,
alive, abandoned on the planet.

**Pays off:** It is on that planet with Vala. Two survivors, both left for dead,
and **Vala may not know this animal killed Jeyin** — she was fighting elsewhere
when it happened.

### Required in this film

- **Include it in Vala's post-credit scene.** The shot is being made anyway and
  the rig exists. Costs one element.
- Shin is never asked whether it should die. That absence is deliberate and she
  may return to it.
- **Archive the rig.**

## P3 — The clasp

**Planted:** Vala gives it to Shin in Scene 22 as a promise.

**RESOLVED 2026-07-30 — it is a clasp, worn as a keepsake.** Built as a functional
fastener off Vala's fighting rig; Shin has nothing to fasten and wears it as a
memento. Call it a **clasp** everywhere. The working design is retained and the
purpose is lost — which is also what has happened to Vala.

**Pays off:**
- Shin wears it for the rest of her life; it is the same piece she wears years later.
- In Film 2 she looks at it **immediately after killing Vala**, and keeps it.

That second beat transforms it. It is not a keepsake from a lost friend — it is
the object she is holding when she realises what she has become, given to her by
the person she just killed. It carries her guilt for the rest of the trilogy.

### Required in this film

- **Design backward** from the piece worn years later, then age it *down*.
- **Build it to survive three productions.** This is the longest-lived hero prop
  in the project.
- **Archive the physical prop.** It must not be struck.
- **Photograph it properly now** — full reference coverage, not a set stills grab.
- It must be legible in close-up and recognisable in silhouette, because it gets
  a close-up in at least two films.

**See:** [`03-characters/shin/`](../03-characters/shin/),
[`03-characters/vala/`](../03-characters/vala/)

## P4 — Baylan and Nyx are brothers in arms

**The blade change is no longer a future plant.** It happens in this film, on
screen, as the blade is withdrawn from Nyx's chest — yellow going in, orange
coming out. See [`03-characters/nyx/`](../03-characters/nyx/).


**Not a plant — a requirement.** Baylan kills Nyx in the finale and **it should
be devastating.**

Which means the friendship has to be *built in this film*, or the death is just
another body in a clearing.

### The evidence is already in the script

| Scene | Beat |
|---|---|
| 7 | Nyx presses him into unloading the ship and making camp; taunts about escapees |
| 10 | The Sabacc game — Nyx wins the pile of chips |
| 14 | **Nyx finds him practicing telekinesis.** They banter about patrols and an upcoming fight, then part to continue duty |
| 20 | On patrol. **Nyx teases him about his blaster** |
| 23 | Nyx volunteers to lead a squad after Vala |

**Nyx is the person Baylan talks to most in the entire film.**

Scene 14 is the load-bearing one, and it is quietly extraordinary: Nyx walks in
on Baylan using the Force and *notices nothing wrong*. They banter. They part
as friends. The person best placed to expose him never looks twice, because he
likes him.

### Required in this film

- **Nyx must be likeable.** Not comic relief, not a thug. The audience has to
  want him to live.
- **The warmth has to be visible**, not implied. The teasing in Scene 20 is
  affection and should play as affection.
- **Nyx is currently a placeholder** with every field TBD. He needs a full
  character package, and he should be prioritised above the other mercenaries.
- Baylan's design already rejects any personal ornament — but if he owns any
  single thing that suggests a relationship, it should relate to Nyx.

**See:** [`03-characters/nyx/`](../03-characters/nyx/),
[`03-characters/baylan/`](../03-characters/baylan/)
