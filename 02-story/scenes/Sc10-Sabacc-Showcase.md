# Scene 10 — the Sabacc game, as a showcase

**The brief changed on 2026-08-01.** This scene is no longer just "establish the
crew". It is the **mercenary cast's scene**: acting, costume, and the reason the
audience should mind when these people die.

Set by the Production Designer, who plays Baylan and has asked that the scene not
be about him.

## Why it is worth the investment

**Every single person at this table is dead by the end of the film.**

| Who | Dies |
|---|---|
| Nyx | Sc.25, Baylan's blade through the chest |
| Shada | Sc.25, the massacre |
| Reya Fenn | Sc.25, the massacre |
| Yaslo Bis | Sc.25, the massacre |
| Merc 4 | Sc.25, the massacre |
| Merc 1, the Wookiee | Sc.24, Vala's grenade — off his own chest rig |
| Merc 2 | Sc.24, the grenade |
| Merc 3 | Sc.24, the grenade |

**This is the only scene in the film where all eight are alive, off duty and
happy.** It is the last time anybody laughs. Nothing else in the film is asked to
carry that, and it is the whole reason the finale can cost something rather than
just resolve.

It is also the scene that makes Yaslo's line in the finale land — *"looks like we
get to do this the fun way"*, said with blasters pointed at a fifteen-year-old, by
a man we watched deal cards and take the mickey out of his mate.

## The problem, measured

As written the scene is **a Nyx scene**:

| Character | Dialogue lines |
|---|---|
| **Nyx** | **14** |
| Reya Fenn | 5 |
| Yaslo Bis | 4 |
| Shada | 3 |
| Merc 1 (Wookiee) | 0 |
| Merc 2 | 0 |
| Merc 3 | 0 |
| Merc 4 | 0 |

Nyx has more than the other three speakers combined, **half the room has nothing
at all**, and every joke is about the same subject — Nyx falling over on Naboo.

That is fine for a scene establishing a crew. It is not a scene that gives eight
performers something to cut a reel from.

## What each performer needs

The rule: **one moment each, and no two of the same kind.** A line, a reaction, or
a piece of business — but something the camera has to come to them for.

### The four who already speak

| Who | What they have | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Nyx** | The whole scene | **Give some away.** He is the most protected character here — he has Sc.7, 14, 20 and a death scene. He can afford to lose four lines |
| **Reya Fenn** | The helmet reveal — *"Her hair tumbles over her shoulders"* — then the killer line | **Already the best entrance in the film.** Protect it. She arrives off patrol, in full armour, and becomes a person in one gesture |
| **Yaslo Bis** | He deals, and lands the biggest laugh | Keep. Dealing gives him business through the whole scene, which is worth more than lines |
| **Shada** | Physical — knuckles Nyx's hair | Her one moment is bodily, and it is the only affection in the film between mercenaries. Keep it and let it play |

### The four with nothing

These are the four the kit was built for, and Sc.10 is the only frame they all
appear in together. **Non-verbal is fine and often stronger** — but furniture is
not.

| Who | Build | Suggested moment | What it also does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Merc 1** — the Wookiee | `merc-1` | Physical comedy of scale in a cramped hold. He cannot fit. Sits on the deck and is still the tallest at the table. Vocalises, no subtitles. **Wins the hand** | **Puts the grenades on his chest rig in frame, ten scenes before Vala takes one.** This is already a hard design requirement and this is where it gets paid |
| **Merc 2** | `merc-2` | Does not play. Sits slightly out, working on the rifle, checking the notches cut into the stock | Shows his personal mark — job tallies — and quietly says what kind of man he is |
| **Merc 3** | `merc-3` | Gets ribbed about a new trophy on his rig. Enjoys it | Shows his personal mark and gets a second voice into the room |
| **Merc 4** | `merc-4` | **The youngest at the table. Laughs a beat behind everyone else. Folds early. Watching more than playing** | **See below — this is the important one** |

### Merc 4 is the one to get right

In Sc.25 he is the mercenary who *"swallows hard, their weapon wavering a little"*
during Krellis's speech — **the only crack in the crew's professionalism in the
whole film.** It currently arrives from nowhere.

**Seed it here.** He is the newest, the least at ease, laughing slightly late
because he is watching the others to know when to. Nobody is unkind to him; he is
just not all the way in yet. Then at gunpoint in front of a child, he is the one
who is not sure.

Two scenes, no dialogue in either, and a complete character.

## Lighting — I got this wrong, and it needs changing

When the scene moved inside I wrote *"A single work lamp swings overhead,
throwing just enough light to see by."* That was written for atmosphere and it is
**the wrong choice for this brief**: one hard moving source with deep falloff will
put half the table in shadow, crush the costume detail, and make eight-way
coverage a nightmare.

**Recommended instead:** more than one motivated source, still salvaged and
practical. A work lamp *plus* the hold's own bulkhead fixtures, plus a light on
the crate itself — something over the cards that gives every face a base level.
Still grimy, still low, still nobody's idea of a lit room. But the costumes are
the second reason this scene exists, and they have to be visible.

## Costume — this is the payoff for the whole kit

The mercenary kit is built on *"personal marks — one kind each"*: worked into the
body, marks of the trade, trophies, kept objects. **Sc.10 is the only place all
four are seen together and therefore the only place that idea is legible.**

If the four builds do not read as four people here, the kit has failed, and this
is where you find out.

Practical: **wide enough to hold the table, tight enough to see a wrist.** The
marks are small.

## Improv — give it a scaffold

Improv on the day works when the frame is fixed and the middle is free. Three
things worth fixing beforehand:

1. **The subject.** Right now every joke is the Naboo job. That is a good spine —
   keep it — but it is one story and eight people. A second running thread
   (somebody is winning too much, somebody owes somebody) gives the others
   something to play.
2. **What each person wants in the scene.** Nyx wants the story corrected. Reya
   wants to sit down and be off duty. Yaslo wants the game moving. Shada wants
   Nyx wound up. Give the silent four one each too, even if it is only "wants the
   hand to end" or "wants to be included".
3. **The out.** The scene has to hand off to Sc.11 — Shin, and the escapees'
   dying fire. **Fix the last beat and improvise up to it**, or the edit will have
   nowhere to cut.

**And keep the takes.** Improv here is exactly what showreels are cut from, and
the material has no other home in the film.

## Baylan should stay out

**Recommendation: out, not lurking.** The lurking option was raised earlier and it
is a good beat in the abstract — but he is the point-of-view character, and an
audience that has followed him for nine scenes will watch him instead of the
crew. Putting him at the edge of frame guarantees the camera and the eye go to
him, which is precisely what this brief is trying to avoid.

**His absence is also doing work.** Jasu is not there either, and the crew
gossips about both of them in consecutive lines — *"Jasu's golden boy"*,
*"Bay's her right hand"*. The two people not at the card game are the captain and
the one who is hiding. That reads better than either of them standing in a corner.

## Revisit this once the mercenaries are built out

**Draft A is at [`drafts/sc10-sabacc-draft-a.fountain`](drafts/sc10-sabacc-draft-a.fountain),
and it is a first pass, not the version to shoot.**

The reason is worth writing down, because it applies to every scene these four
appear in. Draft A gives Mercs 1–4 *business* — a whetstone, a trophy, a winning
hand — and that business was inferred from their **costumes**, because costumes
were all they had. Shada's material works because there is a whole document
behind her and the writing can reach for who she is. The four had nothing behind
them.

That is now fixed: see **"The four, as people"** in
[`03-characters/mercenary-kit/Character.md`](../../03-characters/mercenary-kit/Character.md).
A paragraph each — who they are, how they behave in a room, and what their death
costs.

**Write the next pass from that section, not from the kit list.** The beats will
change, and they should. Merc 2 sitting out of the game reads completely
differently once you know he is the one keeping the count and assumes everybody
else knows it too.

Same applies to casting and to direction on the day.

## Proposed script changes

From the first pass. Draft A implements these; the next pass supersedes them.

1. **Redistribute four of Nyx's fourteen lines** to Merc 2, Merc 3 and Merc 4.
2. **Give Merc 1 a physical beat and a win** — no dialogue, and it puts the
   grenades in frame.
3. **Add a stage direction for Merc 4** laughing a beat late and folding early.
4. **A second running thread** besides the Naboo job.
5. **Fix the closing beat** so the improv has a target to play toward.

Everything else stays. Reya's helmet, Yaslo's deal, Shada's hand in Nyx's hair
and the Naboo argument are the bones of a good scene already.
