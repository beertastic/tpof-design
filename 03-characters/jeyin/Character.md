# Jeyin

> **Production rule:** Refer to the Production Design Bible before any visual development or image generation.

## Art Department Brief

Shin's mother. Injured during the escape and protecting her daughter throughout.
**Killed by Captain Jasu's akk dog in the final scene.**

## Story Function

Jeyin is the loss the film is built around. She is injured early, conceals how
badly from Shin (Scene 18), and keeps moving anyway out of determination to
protect her daughter. In the forest clearing she is killed by the akk dog — the
animal the audience has watched being affectionately petted since Scene 9.

Her death is what leaves Shin with nothing, which is the condition Baylan finds
her in.

## The injury

**She is impaled by torn metal in the crash**, at or just before the film opens.

Scene 2 is the escapees' ship coming down — *"a more rickety ship tumbles through
the evening sky, smoke billowing from its engines."* Jasu briefs her crew about
"a crashed ship nearby" in Scene 9, and Scene 24's set dressing is the
still-swirling column of smoke from it. The event that starts the film is the
event that kills Jeyin.

Currently intended to happen **off screen**, unless the crash can be afforded.

### What kind of wound

- **Penetrating, from dirty torn hull metal.** Not a weapon wound, not a graze,
  not clean. Nobody did this to her.
- **Primal, not technical.** It should read as meat and metal, not as a sci-fi
  injury. No cauterisation, no neat edges, no blaster signature.
- **It goes septic**, because Krellis is a medic with nothing. The visible story
  from Scene 11 onward is infection, not trauma: pallor, sweat, fever-bright
  eyes, grey lips, the colour going out of her.
- **It smells.** Sepsis does. Shin has been living beside it for days — which is
  part of why she cannot sleep, and it connects directly to her *seeing, hearing
  and smelling* the finale.

### She is dying before the akk dog reaches her

This matters and should be played deliberately: **Jeyin is already dying.** The
infection was going to kill her regardless.

They set a monster on a woman who was bleeding out anyway. That does not soften
the killing — it sharpens it into something gratuitous.

### The continuity track

Six scenes of visible progression. Planned, not improvised on the day.

| Scene | State |
|---|---|
| 2 | The crash. Wound taken (off screen) |
| 8 | Keeping up. Concealing it. Nobody is carrying her yet |
| 11 | Asleep — *"pale and sweaty, brow creased in pain."* Infection has set in |
| 16 | Limping. Needs Shin's physical support |
| 17–18 | Hiding the severity from Shin. The limp is the performance |
| 21 | Can barely run |
| 22 | Being pulled along |
| 24A | Cannot run at all. Killed |

The binding gets dirtier and never cleaner. Whatever it was dressed with came
from the ship or from someone's clothing.

### Design consequences

- **The injury is a continuity track, not a detail.** Its visible state must be
  planned per scene: the wound, the binding, her gait, her colour, and how much
  she is concealing.
- She is concealing its severity from Shin (Scene 18), so the costume has to let
  her hide it and let the audience see her hiding it.
- She is killed while unable to run, not while fighting.
- **Death is a creature interaction** — see
  [`08-species/akk-dog/Creature.md`](../../08-species/akk-dog/Creature.md).
  Requires stunt and VFX planning comparable to the `Krellis head shot` already
  flagged in Scene 24.

### The death, precisely

1. Jasu **sets the akk dog on her.** It is an ordered killing.
2. **Shin sees, hears and smells all of it.** She is not shielded from any part.
3. **Jasu calls the dog off.**
4. **Jeyin is still alive.** Shin reaches her and they exchange a few words.
5. She dies.

This is not a kill — it is a **mauling followed by a death scene with dialogue**,
and that is a substantially harder makeup and prosthetics problem than a clean
death:

- The injuries must be **survivable-looking for the duration of the dialogue**
  and unmistakably fatal. She has to be able to speak, and the audience has to
  know she will not stop.
- **Blood continuity** across the mauling, the dialogue and the death needs a
  planned progression, not a single application.
- Shin ends the scene **covered in her mother's blood** — that transfer is a
  costume and continuity requirement for both characters.
- Jeyin is already injured and limping from earlier scenes. The new wounds must
  be distinguishable from the old.
- Prosthetic build required. This is the most demanding makeup in the film.

## Character Overview

**Domestic labour** — a worker, a maid — held alongside her daughter. She
remembers freedom; Shin's own memory of it is deliberately unresolved.

## Costume note

Indoor working clothes: coarse, plain, unfitted, made for scrubbing and carrying
rather than weather. She and Shin are the worst-equipped people in the group and
their footwear was made for floors.

**Her clothing is visibly worse than Shin's.** Everything went to her daughter —
which is both a practical fact and the clearest statement of who she is. See
[`04-factions/slaves-escapees/Faction.md`](../../04-factions/slaves-escapees/Faction.md).

All hand repairs on Shin's costume are Jeyin's work. After the finale, those
patches are all that is left of her.

## Open Questions

- What is the injury, when exactly does it happen, and what is its visible state
  in each of scenes 16, 17, 18, 21, 22 and 24?
- What was she before captivity? She remembers freedom, so there is a life to
  reference — and it should show somewhere.

- What are her last words to Shin? Not an art department decision, but the
  makeup has to let her deliver them — which constrains where the injuries are.
  Nothing may compromise her mouth or throat if she has to speak clearly.
- Escapee costume language overall: what were they issued, what did they steal,
  and what has captivity left them wearing?
