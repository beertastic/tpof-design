# Krellis

> **Production rule:** Refer to the Production Design Bible before any visual development or image generation.

## Art Department Brief

Medic among the escapees; involved in the final confrontation.

## Story Function

**A medic** — poor and badly under-supplied. He met Vala in the arena, where he
treated fighters (Sc.17). He directs the group's route toward **Prodona** — a busy
spaceport city, and the only option besides the woods — and tends Jeyin's injury.

### How he dies, and why it is the film's cruellest beat

**Shot through the head by Captain Jasu in Scene 25, mid-sentence, while
pleading.** Recorded from script v10 — the design documents previously had no
death for him at all.

He is not killed fighting. He steps in front of Shin and Jeyin, draws himself up
to his full height, and argues:

> It doesn't have to be like this. This doesn't have to be who you are. You can
> let us go, let us have the freedom that everyone deserves. You have that power.
> So please just—

> *Krellis stops suddenly. **We pull back through a hole in his head** to find
> Captain Jasu standing beside him, her blaster still glowing. She smiles wickedly
> as Krellis crumples to the floor.*

**The film's one argument for mercy is interrupted by a blaster bolt, and the
person who fires it smiles.** He is a man who cannot fight — he says so himself in
Sc.17, *"I'm really not the fighting type"* — and the last thing he does is try
to talk armed people out of it. It nearly works: the script has one mercenary's
weapon waver while he speaks.

**This is the hardest shot in the film.** A pull-back that begins inside the wound
and widens to find the killer. Method needs deciding early, because it governs how
he is covered through the whole scene. See
[`11-production-tracking/Deaths-And-Effects.md`](../../11-production-tracking/Deaths-And-Effects.md).

**Design consequence:** he must read as a man worth listening to. Large — Shin
remarks on his *"looming stature"* — gentle, and completely unarmoured in every
sense. The size is the joke and the point: the biggest person in the group is the
one who does not fight.

### Backstory — **given by the production 2026-08-02**

**Partial.** More will be answered when he is worked on properly; this is what
exists, recorded so it stops living in a chat window.

He grew up in a **tight-knit family on a war-torn planet**, the **oldest of many
siblings**. He **watched his parents killed by the enemy** — and the reason they
died, as much as he helped, was **the lack of medical support**. So he vowed to
become a medic.

He also had to **learn to navigate danger to keep his family safe**, as he was
now the man of the family. That produced a **fiercely independent, brave,
self-sacrificing** adult, **motivated by love**.

He was **captured protecting his siblings**, and remains a medic — now for the
gladiator arena.

*Interpretation flags, both easy to correct:*

- *"as much as he helped" is read here as **he was there and he tried**, and it
  was not enough because there was no proper help to be had. That reading is what
  makes the vow a penance rather than a career choice. If it meant his own help
  was the problem, this section changes.*
- *"the man of the family" is read as a role he took **as a child**, at the
  moment his parents died, rather than in adulthood.*

### What the backstory decides

**It answers the navigator question the document had left open.** This file
already says the way to Prodona is in his head and nowhere else, and that whether
he has actually *been* there was undecided. The backstory settles it: **navigation
is a childhood survival skill, not local knowledge.** He learned to read danger
and find safe passage for people he was responsible for, long before he ever
heard of Prodona. He is steering by what he has heard, using an instinct for
where it is safe to walk.

That makes his death cost the group more, not less. **They do not lose a map,
they lose a skill** — and a skill cannot be handed over in the seconds he has.

**His death is his origin, repeated exactly.** He became a medic because he
watched people he loved die for want of help, having helped as much as he could.
He dies having stepped in front of Shin and Jeyin, trying to save them with the
only instrument he has left — an argument — and it is not enough either. The vow
he made as a child is broken in the same way it was made. Neither time is he able
to do anything.

**The size and the gentleness now reconcile, which they did not before.** He is
the oldest of many, the man of the family from childhood, and he was captured
**protecting his siblings**. His body has had exactly one use his whole life:
**putting itself between danger and the people behind it.** So the design note
stands and now has a reason — large, gentle, completely unarmoured. And the body
language follows: **he interposes.** Stepping in front of Shin and Jeyin in Sc.25
is the last instance of a lifelong habit, not a moment of courage arriving from
nowhere.

**He is motivated by love, not by politics.** His plea sounds like an argument
about freedom — *"the freedom that everyone deserves"* — but the engine is the
two specific people standing behind him. **It is not a speech.** He is not
addressing an injustice; he is trying to get Shin and Jeyin out alive, and
reaching for the biggest thing he can say.

**It changes what his kit probably is.** The costume note assumes poverty and
improvised instruments. But he is **the arena's medic** — that is a post, with
sanctioned access to whatever the arena provided. So his gear is more likely the
**wreck of a real working kit** than a bag of improvisations: proper instruments,
worn out, unreplaced, and steadily fewer. **The poverty is the arena's, not
his.** That is a different object and a better one — a professional stripped of
supply reads as loss, where an amateur with scraps reads only as poor. Worth
settling before anyone designs the carry, because the carry *is* his design.

**Vala was his patient.** She has twenty years of arena behind her and he was the
medic there; whatever breaks, scars and old injuries she carries, **he is the one
who set them.** That is the answer to *"do he and Vala share anything in their
gear?"* — probably not gear. **The shared history is on her body, not in their
kit**, and it is a continuity note for the art department covering both of them.

### What the backstory opens

- **Where are the siblings now?** He was captured protecting them — did it work?
  Alive, enslaved, dead, or elsewhere? This decides whether he is trying to get
  *back* to someone, and therefore what Prodona actually means to him. It is the
  largest unanswered question about him.
- **Which planet, which war, which enemy?** Deliberately not assumed here — the
  documents should not quietly date the film or hand the war to the Empire.
- **Does anything of home survive on him?** A homeworld marker is exactly the
  kind of small object a person hides through captivity, and he would have had
  reason to.
- **How long was he the arena's medic?** Vala's twenty years is the only clock in
  the room. If he was there for a comparable stretch, his kit's decay has a rate.

## Costume note

A working medic's gear, stripped by poverty. What he has is defined by what he
does **not** have: no proper kit, no supplies, improvised instruments, a bag or
roll that is mostly empty.

Per the Design Bible, occupation must read from silhouette — and for a medic that
means **the carry, not the clothing.** Decide what he salvaged or hid and what it
is carried in; that object is his design.

## He is the navigator

Krellis is the one who knows where they are going, and **the way is in his head
and nowhere else.** He is shot in the clearing.

Whether he has actually been to Prodona or is steering by what he has heard is
open — but either way, the group's only map dies with him.

## Open Questions

- What is his carry — bag, roll, harness — and what is actually in it?
- **Is his kit the wreck of a real professional kit, or improvised?** The
  backstory pushes hard toward the former — he held the post of arena medic, with
  sanctioned access to supplies. Settle this before the carry is designed.
- **Where are his siblings now, and does he think he is going back to them?**
- **Which planet, which war, which enemy** — and does anything of home survive on
  him?
- What did he manage to keep or hide from the arena, and what is improvised?
- He treats Jeyin's injury across scenes 16–22. What can he actually do with
  nothing, and what does that leave visible on her?
- He and Vala met in the arena. Does anything in their gear match?
- `Krellis head shot` (Scene 24) — VFX and prosthetic requirement to be specced.
