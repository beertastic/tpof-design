# Shada

## Art Department Brief

Shada is a compact, highly disciplined mercenary infiltrator whose visual and behavioural language combines a natural predator's patience with the practical habits of a survivor. She is a villain and an integral member of the hunting team, but must remain entertaining, recognisable, and emotionally legible to the audience.

> **Crew reputation:** “Watch your back with her, but she'll always watch yours.”

> **Internal rule:** “Only the hunted are prey.”

> **Visual principle:** She should look like someone who belongs in a wet forest even when standing inside a starship.

## Story Function

Shada is the crew's assassin, thief, scout, and stealth specialist. Because the film is structured around a hunt, she remains near the front of the search: reading terrain, finding access routes, identifying signs of the quarry, infiltrating defended spaces, and striking from advantage.

She is an antagonist rather than a covert hero. Her appeal comes from competence, dry humour, loyalty to the crew, and the contrast between her violent profession and the orderly, community-minded life she might otherwise have lived.

## Design Intent

Shada should feel like a predator shaped by nature rather than a soldier shaped by an institution. She moves economically, rarely wastes energy, and prefers observation, concealment, and timing over spectacle or brute force.

Her small size is part of the crew's affectionate teasing, never a limitation in the design. She knows that reach, mass, and rank mean little when she controls distance, position, and surprise.

She does not crave power. She values order, fairness, competence, and reciprocal loyalty.

## Core Competencies

- Exceptional stealth and infiltration
- Environmental tracking and reconnaissance
- Close-quarters combat when necessary
- Rapid threat and injury assessment
- Constant access to a practical sidearm
- Calm preparation before and after missions

Her hand-to-hand skill is a contingency, not her preferred mode. She would rather avoid a fair fight.

## Moral Code

Shada does not kill indiscriminately. Violence is a tool directed toward the mission's target, not toward the helpless.

When encountering a vulnerable child or non-combatant, she first studies them. She may scent the air, check for weapons and injuries, assess whether they are a threat, and determine whether they have a viable escape. If they can leave safely, she lets them go quietly. If they have been abandoned or cannot survive alone, she takes them with her until they can be left somewhere safe.

This is not sentimental heroism. It is a consistent boundary: only the hunted are prey.

## Life Within the Crew

Shada is comfortable alone but quietly craves family. The mercenary crew has become that family.

Immediately after a mission she performs methodical checks:

- inspects and cleans her blaster
- checks and sharpens her knife
- repairs armour and equipment
- replaces used supplies
- repacks every item in its habitual position

Only when she is ready for the next mission does she relax. She then eats, drinks, and plays sabacc with the team. She is respected, trusted in action, and teased about her height.

## Background

Shada was forced into mercenary life by displacement rather than ambition.

She came from a peaceful, rain-soaked world on the outskirts of Imperial control. During an Imperial resource push, the planet was invaded and its communities displaced. Her people lived in close relationship with the land, working with natural systems rather than attempting to dominate them.

The cultural inspiration should be expressed through values—stewardship, reciprocity, community, discipline, and practical hunting knowledge—not through direct copying of any single Earth culture's dress or sacred imagery.

Without the invasion, Shada might have been a farmer or community hunter. She might also have become a mother and a strong voice within her community, though not its formal leader. She seeks fairness and stable order, not status.

## Physical and Species Notes

Shada is fundamentally human. Her species retains ancient serpentine genetic traits from an earlier evolutionary branch.

The ancestry should remain subtle and plausible:

- visible but restrained scale patterning on selected areas of exposed skin
- heightened sensitivity to scent, vibration, and movement
- patient stillness and economical motion
- few scars, suggesting avoidance and precision rather than invulnerability

Do not turn her into a snake monster. The audience should read her as human first and notice the ancestry second.

## Costume and Armour Language

Apply the Production Design Bible before all character-specific choices.

Shada's silhouette is sleek, compact, and efficient. Her kit has been scavenged, traded, adapted, or taken throughout her mercenary life. Individual parts do not match as a manufactured set, but her maintenance, placement, and repeated modifications create cohesion.

Key rules:

- armour protects vital areas without restricting movement
- exposed skin exists to aid flexibility, heat management, or sensory function—not as decoration
- ship-interface and mission-specific technology is integrated where useful
- nothing rattles, snags, or hangs without purpose
- surfaces are maintained but visibly used
- asymmetry is functional and accumulated
- serpentine ancestry remains visible through selected exposed areas

## Equipment Philosophy

Shada owns few expensive things. She owns important things.

Every retained item has proven its usefulness. Components should show repairs, replaced fasteners, worn contact points, and careful cleaning around mechanisms.

## Hero Props

### Knife

Her knife never leaves her side. It is the single item she would save before any other possession.

It is a survival tool first and weapon second: used for preparing food, cutting material, field repairs, carving markers, and close combat. It should be old, repeatedly sharpened, practical, and unmistakably hers without becoming ornate.

A dedicated hero-prop sheet is required.

### Personal Trinket

Shada carries a small, apparently innocuous reminder of her former home. It should be easy for the audience to miss but consistently present.

Current direction: a worn token, badge, pendant, or braided object made from ordinary material associated with her community. It has no monetary value and should never become an obvious plot device.

Exact form: **Not yet approved.**

## Performance and Body Language

- scans entrances, exits, and changes in the environment
- becomes very still when assessing a threat
- moves with minimal wasted motion
- can transition from solitary focus to relaxed crew intimacy
- does not posture like a conventional soldier
- permits genuine warmth during meals, drinking, and sabacc
- may use scent subtly when gathering information

## Canonical Prompt Framework

Apply the **Production Design Bible v1.0** first.

Create Shada, a compact female mercenary infiltrator and tracker with a fundamentally human appearance and restrained serpentine ancestry. Her silhouette is sleek, efficient, and predator-like rather than military. Fine scale patterning is visible on selected exposed skin, with exposure justified by mobility, heat management, and sensory function. Her armour and technology are scavenged across years of mercenary work: mismatched components adapted into a cohesive, carefully maintained system, including practical ship-interface and mission equipment. Her old survival knife never leaves her side; a simple blaster remains immediately accessible. She has few scars, economical movement, patient stillness, and the alert expression of a hunter who prefers stealth and positioning over a fair fight. Use grounded OT/Andor realism, industrial salvage, believable construction, muted weathered materials, realistic anatomy, and cinematic photography. Avoid Earth pirate, medieval, fantasy, superhero, or ornamental armour language.
