# Shada — Outfit Build Guide

**Infiltration dress — one costume, and the scale armour is the whole design.**

> **THIS FILE IS DERIVED FROM `outfits.yaml`.** That file's `components:` list is
> the source of truth — route, quantity, status and the reasoning for every
> piece. When it changes, this changes. Do not edit this file to record a design
> decision; edit `outfits.yaml` and rewrite this from it.

## Start here — the two things that decide this build

**~440 plates.** That is the hidden cost of the costume and it dwarfs everything
else on this list. Read the plate section before you order anything.

**Pattern the flank panels first.** They are half the plate count, and their area
depends on a pattern nobody has cut yet. Everything else can wait behind them.

## The armour is five pieces, and only five

| Piece | Where | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Gauntlet | her **right** forearm | printed shell |
| Scale cap | her **left** shoulder | made, ~90 plates |
| Scale patch | her **left** outer thigh | made, ~130 plates |
| Flank panels ×2 | laced across the centre front, over the ribs | made, ~110 each |

> **NO BREASTPLATE.** Her chest and sternum stay plain cloth between the panels.
> The armour appears in those five places and nowhere else.

The cap, the patch and the panels all move like heavy cloth. **The gauntlet is
the one exception** — a solid shell that does not flex at all.

## The plates

**Printed ×440. 10 to 15 mm across.** Regular equilateral hexagons, one printed
master repeated — identical in size and outline across the whole costume.

Re-confirmed 2026-08-01 against a generation that came back three to four times
too coarse. **If you can count the plates at a glance, they are wrong.** A patch
should read as a dense field of many tiny pieces, not a few large ones.

**Because they print identical, all the mismatch is painted on.** Cut from
salvaged hull plate, so mismatched alloys oxidising at different rates: dull grey
steel, blackened iron, dark bronze, pale worn pewter, rust-red oxidised iron.

> **NO BRASS AND NO VERDIGRIS.** Within any one patch the metal is broadly
> consistent, with only two or three plates obviously replaced later.

Each plate carries a **raised** serpent ridge — soft, worn, rounded, never
engraved and never a recessed stamp. **Three or four dies repeating across a
panel**, never a unique curl on every plate: it is a manufacturer's mark cast
into cheap hull plate at the mill, industrial and meaningless. What differs
between neighbours is the *wear*, never the mark.

Laid edge to edge, almost touching, a thin dark seam of backing between them.
**Tessellated flat in one plane — never overlapped, never shingled.** It must
never read as carbon fibre or machined hex panelling.

### Plates are missing on purpose

**Five to ten absent from each flank panel, three to five from the cap**, leaving
the hand-cut backing showing through. Bent corners, cracked plates, rows out of
line, an eyelet re-punched beside a torn one, a different cord spliced in.

This is a **finishing instruction, not a licence to make fewer plates.** Lace the
panel complete, then remove — or leave specific positions empty by design. The
gaps fall along the edges, at the waist and over the hip, the places that catch
and flex.

**Scattered absence reads as wear. Regular absence reads as a pattern.** The
labour is unchanged, and the backing behind the gaps now has to be worth looking
at.

## The build

### 1. Hex plates — the raw scale
**Printed ×~440.** See the plate section above. This is the long pole.

### 2. Flank panels — a laced pair, centre front over the ribs
**Made ×2, ~110 plates each.** **Pattern these first.**

Unmatched to each other. Laced and riveted onto flexible hand-cut
leather-and-fabric backing — irregular, never a neat manufactured panel. The
lacing is fine and mostly hidden behind the plates; the flex comes from the gaps
and the lacing.

### 3. Scale cap — her LEFT shoulder
**Made ×1, ~90 plates.** **A palm's width — the point of the shoulder only.**
Loose and scavenged. Three to five plates absent.

### 4. Scale patch — her LEFT outer thigh
**Made ×1, ~130 plates.** **Dark bronze — the one warm thing in the entire
costume**, and the only place that colour appears.

### 5. Gauntlet — her RIGHT forearm
**Printed ×1.** A solid shell, dull grey steel, with a cluster of dim amber
telltales at the wrist.

> **It must NOT match the other four pieces.**

Printing it is worth **~150 hand-laced plates**, each of which would have needed
two punched holes. It is the largest single saving in this build and it survives
every revision, because it is a property of the piece rather than of the coverage.

### 6. Vest — fitted, sleeveless, dusty grey
**Bought ×1. Heavy close-woven CLOTH, not hide** — changed 2026-08-02, which
turned this from a hide to find into a garment to source and distress.

> **The serpentine grain is a WEAVE, not a pressing.** It has to be found in the
> cloth at purchase or it does not exist — there is no equivalent of embossing a
> hide. Look for a fine, close, irregular texture woven in, tonal and readable
> only in raking light. **Not a reptile print**, which is what the market will
> offer you.

### 7. Belts — waist, and a second slung diagonally below
**Bought ×2.** Worn taupe leather. See the dyeing note under boots.

### 8. Harness buckles and fittings
**Bought ×6 — real hardware, not printed.**

> Printed buckles fail at the layer lines, under exactly the loads a costume puts
> on them.

Real load-rated buckles also read *more* correctly, not less: the Design Bible
calls for industrial salvage over fine modern hardware. Printing is for buckles
that are seen but not loaded.

### 9. Holster and knife sheath — her RIGHT thigh
**Made ×2.** Taupe leather, on the drawing side. Built around the WESTAR-35, not
around a generic pistol.

### 10. WESTAR-35 blaster pistol
**Printed ×1.** On her **right** side, hip or thigh. Well used, never factory
fresh.

### 11. Combat knife
**Printed ×1.** On her **left** hip. Well used.

### 12. Boots — mid-calf, grey-brown taupe
**Bought ×1.** Long leather straps wound criss-cross up the shaft.

> **Taupe is a dyeing and distressing job.** Belts, holster, thigh strap, boots
> and the gauntlet cuff all read warm brown as bought. The costume drifts back
> toward brown on almost every image, and it will do the same on the bench.

### 13. Scale make-up
**Made ×1.** Fine scales on the exposed skin of her face, neck, collarbone,
shoulders, arms and hands. A separate discipline from the armour.

**The reptilian pupil is a CONTACT LENS on a human performer** — the eye keeps
its human shape, its lids, its lashes and its expression, and only the pupil is
wrong. It is the single highest-value element in her make-up. Locked against
`scale_portrait.png`, not against the costume reference.

## Palette

Muted forest throughout — **dusty stone grey, grey-green, khaki and taupe.**
Matte and low-noise, with nothing that would snag or rattle. All leather in
grey-brown taupe.

**The dark bronze thigh patch is the one warm thing in the costume.**

Metallic but never bright: dulled, scratched, weathered to a low sheen, catching
light in small dull glints across many facets.

## What it is not

Do **not** make the armour leather. Do **not** make it a printed pattern. Do
**not** make it forged or ceremonial armour.

The costume is **close-fitting and follows her figure** — waist defined, cut to
the body. She is compact, agile and quick and the clothes must say so: nothing
bulky, nothing loose, no oversized jacket swallowing her silhouette. Exposed skin
at the shoulders and upper arms is functional, and it is what makes her inherited
scales legible.

It is a working costume built for infiltration, not display. **Everything on it
does a job.** Nothing polished, ornamental or factory fresh.
