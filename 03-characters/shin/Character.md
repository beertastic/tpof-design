# Shin

> **Production rule:** Refer to the Production Design Bible before any visual development or image generation.

> **Co-lead.** Shin is not a supporting escapee. This film is the origin of her
> partnership with Baylan, and it is the first of three planned films before the
> point at which the two of them are already established. Her design here is the
> start of a long arc and must be built to travel.

## Art Department Brief

Fifteen years old. A captive who has escaped with her mother Jeyin and a small
band of prisoners, running through a forest world while mercenaries hunt them.

She is Force-sensitive and **does not know it.** Things are happening to her — a
shadow that pulses around her, visions that arrive uninvited — and she has no
framework for any of it. She thinks something is wrong with her, and she tells
nobody, not even her mother.

By the end of the film her mother is dead and a stranger who understands exactly
what she is has appeared in the clearing. That is why she goes with him.

## Story Function

Shin is the film's other half. Baylan is a man hiding from what he is; Shin is a
girl who does not know what she is. They have visions of each other before they
ever meet, and the film ends with them in the same place.

She is also, crucially, **not hardened.** Scene 17 has her *horrified* by Vala's
account of the arena. At fifteen and in captivity she has still been sheltered
from the worst of it — which is what Jeyin has been spending her life doing, and
what Jeyin dies doing.

## Design Intent

**1. She is the only colour in the film.** The actress has long red hair, styled
toward a Star Wars silhouette — *pretty, but never sexy.* Against charcoal, dust
brown and faded olive in a wet dark forest, that hair is the brightest thing in
any frame she occupies. Do not fight it. It makes a fifteen-year-old findable in
an ensemble of twelve, instantly, without a single compositional trick.

It also sets up the loss. **The trauma of this story leads her to cut it** — and
when she does, the film loses its only colour. Design toward that.

**2. She is a child in an adult catastrophe.** Not a young warrior, not a
prodigy, not capable. She is fifteen, exhausted, filthy and frightened, being
carried by people who love her. Any design that makes her look competent or
dangerous is wrong for *this* film — that is what the next two are for.

**3. Everything is wrong-sized and second-hand.** Nothing she wears was made for
her or chosen by her. Captivity clothing, adjusted by her mother, worn to
destruction on a forest run.

## Physical Design

- **Species:** Human
- **Age:** 15
- **Build:** Slight and underfed. Not athletic, not frail — a child who has not
  had enough food for a long time
- **Hair:** **Long and red.** The dominant element of her design and the only
  strong colour in the film. Styled toward a Star Wars silhouette — braided,
  bound or wrapped for practicality, never loose and glamorous. **Pretty, never
  sexy.** She is fifteen, and this is non-negotiable
- **Skin:** Pale. See *Open Questions* — how pale depends on whether she has ever
  lived outside
- **Face:** Dirt-smeared through most of the film (Scene 11 is explicit). Young,
  open, and readable in a way nobody else in the film is — she has never had to
  hide her feelings from anyone, except about the visions
- **Hands:** Work-marked but small. She has been used for labour, never for
  fighting

### The hair, specifically

Practical rules, so it survives contact with the story:

- **Bound, braided or wrapped** for the forest run. A fifteen-year-old fleeing
  through wet undergrowth does not have loose hair.
- **Filthy** by Scene 11, and it stays that way.
- It should catch and hold what little light there is — firelight in Scene 11,
  dawn in Scene 16 — because that is what makes her findable.
- It is the **continuity clock** of the film: how bound, how dirty, how damaged.

## Costume and Armour

The breakdown names two garments: a **"Scraggly Top"** (Scene 16) and a
**"Tunic"** (Scene 22). Treat these as one costume progressively destroyed, not
as two changes.

Escapee costume language is the inverse of the mercenaries': not what she chose,
but what she was issued or could steal. Coarse undyed or badly-dyed cloth, no
fastening that cost anything, no pockets worth having, nothing fitted. Repairs
made by hand with whatever was available — and those repairs are Jeyin's work,
not hers.

Footwear is the critical detail. Whatever she has was not made for crossing a
forest, and by Scene 21 it should be failing.

**Nothing about the costume may glamorise her.** No shaping, no exposed midriff,
no artful tearing. She is a filthy, frightened fifteen-year-old and the costume
must say so.

## Equipment and Hero Props

### The clasp

**Vala gives Shin a clasp in Scene 22, as a promise, immediately before walking
away to draw the hunters off.**

It is not magical. It is special to Vala — the one thing she has — and giving it
away is what a person does when they do not expect to come back.

**It is the same piece Shin wears years later.** This scene is the origin of a
known object, so the clasp must be designed backward from that piece: match it,
then age it *down* to what it looked like before years of wear.

- Metal, small, functional. It fastens something.
- Worn smooth at the contact points — it has closed something thousands of times.
- No ornament that reads as jewellery. It is a fastener with a history.
- Legible in close-up in Scene 22, and recognisable years later.
- Design in parallel with Baylan's crystal pouch. Both are small objects worn
  against the body; both are the last thing left of a person; both travel into
  the next film.

### Everything else

Effectively nothing. She is a fleeing captive — whatever she has was grabbed,
given, or is part of what she was already wearing. The emptiness of her kit is
the characterisation, and it sits against Baylan, who has *deliberately* owned
nothing that says anything about him, while she has nothing at all.

## Performance and Body Language

- **She looks up.** Nobody else in this film looks at the sky. She does, and it
  is the clearest single behaviour she has.
- **She is carried, and helps carry.** Scene 16 has her supporting her injured
  mother; the group is protecting her throughout.
- **She hides the visions.** She tells nobody. Watch for the moment she almost
  does, and doesn't.
- **She cannot sleep** (Scene 11), and it should show cumulatively — more
  exhausted in every successive scene.
- **She is not brave yet.** Fear reads plainly on her; she has never had to
  perform composure.
- **She looks directly into camera twice** (Scenes 6 and 12). What that means is
  a directorial decision, but it is the only time in the film anyone does.

## Open Questions

- **When does she cut her hair — in this film, or between films?** The most
  urgent question. If it happens here, the prompt pack needs a shorn variant and
  the costume must work both ways. If it happens in Film 2, this film ends with
  the hair intact and the loss lands later.
- **Does Shin remember freedom?** Jeyin does. Shin's own memory is deliberately
  unresolved, and it sets her pallor, whether she squints in daylight, and
  whether Scene 8 is wonder or recognition. Designed to the middle for now:
  markedly pale, but not someone who has never seen the sun.
- **Does she witness her mother's death, or find her afterwards?** Determines
  whether Shin and the akk dog are ever in frame together.
- What is Scene 12 exactly — she sees a vision *and* stands within it. Is she
  watching herself? A VFX and staging decision.
- Does Baylan speak to her in Scene 24A, or is the meeting silent?
- Escapee faction costume language needs settling; her garments must sit inside it.
