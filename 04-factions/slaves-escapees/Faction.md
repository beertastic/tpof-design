# Slaves and Escapees

> Refer to the Production Design Bible before all visual development.

> **Label confirmed 2026-07-30:** the faction is called **Slaves / Escapees** —
> both, because the captivity is as much their identity as the escape. Not
> renamed, not split.

## Narrative Function

Escaped prisoners attempting to cross the forest world and reach safety, hunted
by Captain Jasu's mercenaries. Four are named; the rest are background.

## Where they came from — the auction

**They escaped from a slave auction.** An accident during the sale triggered a
mass revolt; dozens broke out. **These four found each other in the mayhem and
stole a ship** — the rickety vessel that comes down in Scene 2.

This is the single most useful fact about the faction, and it settles several
design questions at once.

### They share an escape, not an origin

People at auction are brought together from different owners and different lots.
The four have no common history and no common holding:

- **Jeyin and Shin** are mother and daughter, together throughout.
- **Vala and Krellis** already knew each other — he was the medic in the arena
  where she was made to fight.
- Beyond those two pairs, nothing connects them but the day they ran.

**Therefore: no shared brand, tag or marking.** They did not come from the same
place, so nothing was done to all of them. The group reads as a group through
*condition and behaviour* — years of wear, hand repair, wrong sizing, indoor
clothing destroyed by a forest — not through anything issued.

That also keeps the four occupational silhouettes fully distinct, avoids an
institutional read, and costs nothing in daily makeup.

### They were being sold

This is worse than being held, and it changes the costume logic:

- **People at auction are presented.** Cleaned up, made saleable. That is very
  likely why **Shin's clothing was the best-kept in the group** — she was being
  prepared to be sold, which is the grooming subtext arriving from a new
  direction and confirming it.
- Jeyin's correspondingly worse condition may be her own doing *and* the
  auction's assessment of her relative value.
- Whatever presentation was done to them is now days old and being destroyed by
  the forest. What survives is the **shape** of having been made presentable.

### The ship is stolen

Not theirs, not chosen, taken in the chaos — which is exactly why it is
*rickety* and why it comes down. They took whatever would fly.

See [`07-vehicles/`](../../07-vehicles/).

### Where they are going

**Prodona** — a busy spaceport city on this planet, named by Krellis in Scene 8.

Not a haven. Nobody is waiting for them. It is simply large enough to disappear
into, and it is **the only option besides the woods.** Freedom, for these people,
means a crowd big enough that nobody looks at you twice.

It also puts a clock on the mercenaries: if the escapees reach a working
spaceport they are untraceable within the hour, so the pursuit is a race and the
forest is the last place they can be caught.

They never get there. See [`07-locations/prodona/`](../../07-locations/prodona/).

### The other escapees

**Dozens got out.** The "Group Of Escaped Prisoners" in Scene 8 are some of them.
Others are unaccounted for, which means the mercenaries are not necessarily
hunting only these four — and that there are survivors elsewhere in the galaxy
for later films.

## The core rule — occupation, not uniform

**These people are not prisoners in prison clothing. They are labour, and their
clothes are the working gear of whatever they were used for.**

There is no shared uniform and no institutional garment. What unites them is
condition, not cut: everything is years old, never replaced, repaired by hand,
and now being destroyed by a forest crossing they were never equipped for.

This is the Production Design Bible's *occupation shapes appearance* rule
(§2) applied to people who did not choose their occupation. It gives four named
characters four distinct silhouettes while keeping them unmistakably one group.

### The inversion

The mercenary faction wears **what it chose** — salvaged, personalised, decorated,
assembled over years by people with agency and money.

The escapees wear **what they were given for someone else's purpose.** Nothing
was chosen. Nothing fits properly. Nothing is decorated, because decoration
implies ownership of yourself.

That opposition should be legible in any frame containing both groups. It is the
faction's single most important visual idea.

## Shared condition

Whatever the garment, all of it shows:

- **Years of continuous wear.** Never replaced, only repaired.
- **Hand repair, not workshop repair.** Mismatched thread, poor light, no proper
  tools. The repairs are done *by* the wearers or for each other.
- **Wrong sizing.** Issued, inherited or handed down. Nothing was cut for this body.
- **No fastenings worth anything.** Cord, tied cloth, bone or scrap. Nothing
  manufactured, nothing that could be sold or sharpened.
- **Forest damage on top of everything else.** Mud, water, tearing, failing
  footwear. Days of running through wet undergrowth in clothing meant for indoor
  work or an arena floor.
- **No colour.** Undyed, badly dyed, or dye long since gone. The palette sits at
  the bone-white, dust-brown and ash end of the Bible's range.

## Per-occupation language

### Domestic labour — Jeyin and Shin

Indoor working clothes. Coarse, plain, unfitted, made for scrubbing and carrying
rather than for weather. Aprons, wraps, tied sleeves — whatever a household or
facility issues to people it does not think about.

Critically: **these clothes have no business outdoors.** Jeyin and Shin are the
worst-equipped people in the group. Their footwear was made for floors. This is
why Shin's is failing by Scene 21, and it should be visibly the case from Scene 8.

### Arena — Vala

The remnants of a fighting rig. Not armour and not costume — the functional
minimum an arena gives someone it expects to lose: strapping, wraps, a belt or
harness, reinforcement at the forearms and shins.

Hers is the only clothing in the group **designed to take damage**, which makes
her the best-equipped for a forest and reads instantly as different from the
others. It should also be the source of the clasp she gives Shin in Scene 22 —
see her character document.

Avoid gladiator and Roman reference entirely; the Bible rejects it explicitly.
This is industrial, strapped and practical, not classical.

### Medical — Krellis

A working medic's gear, stripped by poverty. Whatever he has is defined by what
he *doesn't* have: no proper kit, no supplies, improvised instruments, a bag or
roll that is mostly empty.

His occupation must read from silhouette per the Bible — and for a medic that
means the carry, not the clothing. Decide what he salvaged or hid, and what it is
carried in. He met Vala in the arena, so his gear may carry that history too.

## Shin — a note on the subtext

The production's intent is that **Shin was being groomed.** This is implied, never
stated, and it is not depicted.

The art department carries it through **incongruity, not through her body:**

- Her clothing was the **best-kept in the group** — cleaner, less worn, more
  intact than her mother's — because she was being kept presentable for someone.
- By the time we meet her that advantage is destroyed. What remains is the
  *shape* of something that used to be looked after.
- Jeyin's clothing is correspondingly worse, because everything went to her
  daughter.

**This makes the no-sexualisation rule more binding, not less.** The horror is
entirely in what someone else intended and what her mother is fighting. Nothing
about it may appear on the character. No fitted cut, no exposed skin as design
language, no adult styling, no suggestive framing, ever. If an image could be
read that way, it is wrong regardless of what else it gets right.

See [`03-characters/shin/Character-Lock.md`](../../03-characters/shin/Character-Lock.md).

## Rejections

- No institutional prison uniform, no stripes, no numbers, no shackles as costume.
- No Earth-historical slavery reference of any period. This is the Bible's
  Earth-influence rule and it applies with particular force here.
- No gladiator, Roman or classical language for Vala.
- No rags-as-fashion. Damage follows use and the route they ran, not a designer's
  hand.
- No character is sexualised. Shin's protection is absolute; the same principle
  applies to Vala, whose arena history must never be used as licence.
- Nothing decorative. These people own nothing.
