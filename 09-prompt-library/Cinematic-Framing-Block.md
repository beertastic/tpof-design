# Cinematic Framing Block

Canonical source for the shared framing language used by **narrative slots only**
— in-scene frames, not reference plates. Inline it into each character's
`Prompts.md` the same way as the Style and Capture blocks.

**Do NOT paste this with turnarounds, material plates, prop plates or study
strips.** Those want flat, even documentation light and the whole subject in
frame. Shallow depth of field and an off-centre crop on a materials board is a
fault, not a style.

---

## Why this exists

Described only by its content, a narrative image comes back **centred,
eye-level, flat-lit, both subjects complete and nothing in front of or behind
them.** That is a reference plate with scenery, and it is what every narrative
frame in this project looked like until 2026-08-03.

The Realism and Capture blocks were already strong — anamorphic glass, 35 mm
negative, halation, grain in the midtones. Adding more language in that register
moved nothing. **What was missing was composition and motivated light**, and
adding the three paragraphs below produced the first frame in the project that
reads as a film still rather than a costume record.

---

## Block — Cinematic Framing

> **CAMERA.** Not at chest height and not centred. Put the lens where a camera
> operator would put it for this moment — low if the subject is low, high if the
> geography matters. The subject sits OFF CENTRE and the frame is not balanced.
> Something may run out of frame: a trailing arm, the hindquarters of an animal,
> the top of a tree. **A film frame crops. A reference plate does not.**
>
> **LIGHT, MOTIVATED AND DIRECTIONAL.** Name where it comes from and let it do
> something — low sun raking through trees from behind and to one side, a shaft
> through a gap, firelight from below, a work lamp off frame. Catch a hard rim on
> a wet edge and throw long shadows across the ground. Faces sit in bounce rather
> than in the source. **Flat overcast light from nowhere is what a reference plate
> looks like.**
>
> **DEPTH IN THREE LAYERS.** Something out of focus across a near corner — ferns,
> a branch, a shoulder. The subject sharp in the middle. Separation behind, with
> atmosphere BETWEEN the layers and not merely behind them. Anamorphic glass
> earns its keep in the foreground, and a frame with nothing in front of the
> subject has nothing to render.

---

## Notes for whoever uses it

**The depth paragraph is the one people cut, and it is the one that works.** Oval
anamorphic bokeh only appears where there is an out-of-focus highlight, so a
prompt that asks for anamorphic character while keeping the foreground empty has
asked for something it made impossible.

**"A film frame crops" is doing real work.** Generators default to fitting the
whole subject in shot, because most of their training for "character in costume"
is exactly that. Permission to crop has to be explicit.

**Keep it out of the plate slots.** In this project only six of sixteen numbered
slots are narrative. Check applicability before inlining — see the `Applies to
slots …` convention in each `Prompts.md`.
