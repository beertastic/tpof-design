# Deaths and Effects

Every death in the film, who does it, whether the camera sees it, and what it
costs to build. Derived from screenplay v10.

**Eleven people die. Twelve if Vala does, and the script never says.**

The design documents had four of these wrong — see
[`Script-v9-Reconciliation.md`](Script-v9-Reconciliation.md).

## The headline: only two deaths are expensive

| Death | Camera | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Krellis | **Fully staged** | **High** — one specific VFX shot |
| Jeyin | **Fully staged, extended** | **Highest** — prosthetics plus creature interaction |
| Nyx | Fully staged | Medium — blade rig and a colour change |
| Captain Jasu | Fully staged | Low |
| Merc 1, 2, 3 | **Cut away before it happens** | **Near zero** |
| Shada, Reya Fenn, Yaslo Bis, Merc 4 | **Played on Shin's face** | **Near zero** |
| Vala | Never shown | Zero |

**Seven of the eleven deaths cost almost nothing**, and that is the script's
doing rather than a compromise. Worth knowing before anything is budgeted.

## Scene 24 — the grenade. Merc 1, Merc 2, Merc 3

The Wookiee has Vala off her feet. She takes a thermal detonator from his own
chest rig, opens her hand, and:

> **NYX:** Oh shi-
>
> **> CUT TO:**

**The camera leaves before the detonation.** The explosion is heard in Sc.25 by
three people somewhere else. Nothing is staged, nothing is built, nobody is made
up.

**Vala is not confirmed dead** — see below.

**Requires:** a detonator prop that reads clearly on the Wookiee's rig **from
Scene 10**, ten scenes earlier, or the payoff plays as convenient.

## Scene 25 — Krellis. Shot by Captain Jasu

> Krellis stops suddenly. **We pull back through a hole in his head** to find
> Captain Jasu standing beside him, her blaster still glowing. She smiles wickedly
> as Krellis crumples to the floor.

**The most technically specific direction in the screenplay**, and the single
hardest shot in the film. It is a pull-back that starts inside the wound and
widens to find the killer — a reveal built out of the injury itself.

**It is also the cruellest beat in the film and that is structural.** He is killed
*mid-sentence*, mid-plea, arguing that the mercenaries could choose to be
something else — *"You can let us go, let us have the freedom that everyone
deserves. You have that power. So please just—"*. The film's one argument for
mercy is interrupted by a blaster bolt, and the person who fires it smiles.

**Requires:**

- A **head wound appliance** that survives a camera move toward it, not just a
  wide.
- **The shot itself.** Almost certainly VFX, or a match cut between a practical
  hole and a widening plate. Needs deciding early — it affects how Krellis is
  shot all through the scene.
- Jasu's **blaster still glowing** — practical or added.

**Design consequence for Jasu:** she does not shoot him from cover or at
distance. She walks up beside a man making a speech and puts a bolt through his
head while he is still talking, then smiles. Everything else about her should be
built to make that land as *characteristic*, not shocking.

## Scene 25 — Jeyin. Killed by the akk dog, on Jasu's command

Two stages, and they have different requirements.

**The mauling.** Jasu whistles, the beast *"lands on top of Jeyin, scratching
with its thick claws and snapping at her flesh. Jeyin screams."* Then a second
whistle and it comes off her. **Shin is frozen, forced to watch.**

**The dying.** *"The injuries the older woman has sustained are awful. She only
has a few moments before she bleeds out."* She then speaks to her daughter, and
the last line of the film's spine comes out broken:

> **JEYIN:** Shin. It's alright. *(shaky breath)* You...control...it.

**This is the most demanding practical work in the production**, and it was
already flagged before anyone read the script:

- The injuries must read **survivable for the length of her dialogue and
  unmistakably fatal**. Those pull in opposite directions and the whole scene
  rests on getting the balance right.
- The akk dog is a **built digital asset**, so this is creature animation
  interacting with a performer on the ground — contact, weight, and the
  performer's body being moved by something that is not there.
- Blood continuity from the mauling through several minutes of screen time,
  across her death and Shin's Force blast.

**Note the wound already on her.** She has been bleeding through her top since
Sc.16 from the crash. The mauling lands on a woman who is already dying — the
design documents call it a six-scene continuity track and the script supports it.

## Scene 25 — Captain Jasu. Killed by Nyx, through Baylan

> A blaster fires from behind them and, without looking, **Bay deflects it from
> his lightsaber. It hits Jasu in the chest**, and she falls to the ground,
> unmoving.

**Nyx kills his own captain by accident, through the man he is shooting at.**
Nothing in the design documents knew this — they had Baylan killing her.

Cheap to stage: a chest hit and a fall. The cost is the **deflection**, which is a
VFX bolt path off a blade that is already a VFX element.

**Nobody in the film ever remarks on it.** Nyx does not appear to realise, and he
is dead a page later.

## Scene 25 — Nyx. Killed by Baylan

> Nyx's face is slack. His knife drops from his hand. He looks down. **The tip of
> Bay's lightsaber protrudes from his chest.** Bay pulls it out. **It is a darker
> colour now, more orange than yellow.**

**Corrected:** the design documents had Nyx killed *last*. He is not — he dies
**before** the massacre. Baylan stabs him, kneels with him while he dies, speaks
to Shin, and only then turns on the others.

**Requires:**

- **The blade through the chest.** Practical rig or VFX, and it is held long
  enough to be read.
- **The colour change**, yellow to orange, **during the withdrawal**. This is the
  film's only statement about what Baylan has become, and it happens on this shot.
  It must be unmissable and must not look like a lighting change.

**Also corrected:** the documents call for Nyx's raised hands to read as a Force
push, a deliberate misdirect. **That is not in v10** — he pulls a knife and
advances on Shin. If the misdirect is wanted, it is a script change.

## Scene 25 — the massacre. Shada, Reya Fenn, Yaslo Bis, Merc 4

> He whirls into battle, **cutting down his ex-comrades in a flurry of light and
> screams.** Shin, watching at first, **scrunches her eyes shut.** But, unable to
> stop herself, she slowly opens one of her eyes to see Baylan, an unstoppable
> force.

**Four deaths, and the camera is on the fifteen-year-old.**

The art department recommendation on file — *"play it on her face; her lack of
fear is the shot"* — is not a compromise to argue for. **It is what the script
already does.** Shin closes her eyes, and the audience gets light, sound and one
opened eye.

**Requires:** sound design, blade light, and a performance. No wounds, no
appliances, no blood on four bodies. It is the cheapest sequence in the film and
the most frightening, for the same reason.

**Do not "improve" this by shooting the kills.** Four staged lightsaber deaths
would cost more and land softer.

## Vala — never shown, never confirmed

The camera cuts away before the detonator goes off. Her death exists only as
reported speech, from a grieving man who saw nothing and a hostile one who
survived the same blast.

**No death to stage, no injury to build.** The standing rule — *do not stage a
death, show no injury* — is already what the script does. See
[`Script-v9-Reconciliation.md`](Script-v9-Reconciliation.md) §2.

**Baylan kills five.** Nyx, then the four in the massacre. Not Jasu.

## The akk dog does not die, and the mechanism changed

The design documents have it *"knocked out, left alive and abandoned"*. In v10 it
is knocked back by Shin's blast — *"The beast jumps in front of Jasu, absorbing
most of the impact"* — and then:

> Bay locks eyes with the beast and **something passes between them**. He nods
> towards the woods, and the pet turns and runs away.

**He does not spare it. He dismisses it, and it obeys him rather than Jasu.**
That is a better beat than the one on file, and it is set up in Sc.9 when the
animal leaves Jasu and lies down beside him.

**Requires:** creature animation for a silent exchange and an exit. No injury.

## Open

- **The Krellis shot.** Decide the method early — it governs how he is covered
  through the whole scene.
- **Jeyin's appliance.** Survivable-looking for the length of her dialogue,
  unmistakably fatal. The most demanding build in the film.
- **The blade colour change** — how it is achieved, and how it is protected from
  reading as a lighting shift.
- **Nyx's raised hands.** In the documents, not in the script. Script change or
  drop it.
